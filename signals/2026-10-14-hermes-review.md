## 3. Hidden Risks  

- **Sector concentration**:  
  - **SDMU**, **BRPT**, **KOTA**, **CBDK**, **BREN**, **RAJA**, **PTRO** are all heavily weighted toward *resource‑related* sectors (mining, coal, metals). Roughly 60 % of the list sits in commodities‑exposed stocks, creating a sector‑bias risk if global commodity sentiment turns bearish.  
- **Liquidity risk**:  
  - Several low‑price tickers (**CBDK** at ~3,740 IDR, **GOTO** at ~28 IDR, **BNBR** at ~64 IDR) are likely thinly traded. Position sizing at the suggested 5‑20 day horizon could easily exceed daily volume, leading to slippage and execution risk.  
- **Correlation risk**:  
  - The majority of picks are driven by the same *RSI‑oversold* signal. This creates a hidden correlation cluster; a market‑wide rebound or failure of the oversold bounce will affect most positions simultaneously.  
- **Timing / chase risk**:  
  - **SDMU**, **BRPT**, **CBDK** already exhibited >2 % price jumps (volume breakout). Entering at the top of the entry band may result in immediate pull‑back, especially if the breakout was a short‑term spike rather than a sustained trend.  
- **Stale data / regime shift**:  
  - The “Historical edge” metric is based on the last 10‑20 trades, but there is no disclosure of the look‑back window (e.g., last 6 months vs. last 2 years). If the market regime has shifted (e.g., post‑COVID volatility regime), the edge may be overstated.  
- **Indicator overlap**:  
  - All picks rely on either RSI‑oversold or volume‑breakout. No diversification across other technical or fundamental signals (e.g., moving‑average cross, earnings surprise). This redundancy inflates the apparent “high‑tier” signal strength while actually representing a single‑source bias.  

## 4. What the Author Got Right  

The author correctly identified that a pronounced RSI‑oversold condition can generate short‑term mean‑reversion opportunities, and the back‑tested edge percentages (e.g., 9.30 % for WIFI, 7.84 % for SDMU) demonstrate that the signal has historically produced a positive expectancy when applied to a sufficiently large sample of trades.  

## 5. Critical Recommendations  

1. **Re‑calibrate stop‑loss levels** – Replace the flat ‑3 %/‑4 %/‑5 % stops with structure‑based stops (e.g., below the most recent swing low, ATR‑based multiples, or breach of a key support zone). This will align risk with actual market volatility and prevent arbitrary stop placement.  
2. **Prune low‑R/R, low‑win‑rate picks** – Remove **GOTO**, **BNBR**, and **PTRO** (R/R ≈ 1, win‑rates ≤ 40 %) from the *Buy* list or downgrade them to *Watch* status. Their inclusion dilutes the overall portfolio quality and inflates tail‑risk.  
3. **Diversify signal sources and sector exposure** – Add at least a handful of picks derived from complementary indicators (e.g., MACD cross, earnings‑beat momentum, or sector‑relative strength) and ensure that no more than ~30 % of the portfolio is allocated to commodity‑heavy stocks. This will mitigate correlation risk and improve the robustness of the strategy.
