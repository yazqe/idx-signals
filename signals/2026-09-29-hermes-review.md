## 3. Hidden Risks  

- **Sector concentration** – No sector breakdown is provided. Several symbols (WIFI, WMUU, ESIP, MINA) are likely in the same high‑growth/technology cluster, risking a sector‑specific reversal wiping out multiple positions simultaneously.  

- **Liquidity risk** – Many low‑price stocks (e.g., ASHA at ≈ IDR 37, GOTO at ≈ IDR 37, BNBR at ≈ IDR 69) often have thin average daily volume. A 5 % move could easily trigger slippage, especially if the position size is not scaled to turnover.  

- **Correlation / strategy overlap** – All picks are generated from a single “RSI‑oversold” trigger. This creates a highly correlated basket; a rebound in market sentiment that invalidates the mean‑reversion premise will hurt the entire portfolio simultaneously.  

- **Timing / chase risk** – The entry zones are set at the current price level, meaning the trader is buying *after* the RSI has already plunged. If the price has already “bottomed out,” the upside left may be limited, while the downside risk (gap‑down, further oversold pressure) is ignored.  

- **Stale data / regime shift** – Historical edge figures are based on the last 9‑13 trades for each ticker. No mention is made of the time window (e.g., last 6 months vs. last 2 years). If market regimes have shifted (e.g., from a bullish to a risk‑off environment), the edge may be overstated.  

- **Indicator redundancy** – RSI oversold is the sole signal; no confirmation from volume, trend‑strength, or higher‑timeframe bias is used. The lack of independent confluence inflates confidence artificially.
