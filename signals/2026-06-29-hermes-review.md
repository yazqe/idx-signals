## 3. Hidden Risks

- **Sector concentration**: Both tickers are described only as “low‑tier” securities. If they belong to the same sector (e.g., small‑cap consumer or mining), the portfolio could be unintentionally **sector‑biased**. Without sector tags, the risk of a sector‑wide shock is hidden.  
- **Liquidity risk**: Low‑tier tickers often have **average daily volume < 100 k shares**. Entering a position sized for a typical 1 % portfolio exposure could easily move the market, especially with a 2 % stop that may be hit by normal intraday volatility.  
- **Correlation / signal overlap**: Both picks rely solely on the **RSI‑oversold** signal. This creates a **false confluence** – the two trades are not independent; a rebound in one will likely coincide with the other, inflating apparent diversification.  
- **Timing / chase risk**: If either ticker has already **gapped up > 10 %** today to reach the entry zone, the RSI may already be in the rebound phase, reducing the upside and increasing the chance of a **re‑test of the stop**. The analysis does not check recent price action.  
- **Stale indicator**: RSI is a **momentum‑lagging** oscillator. Using a single‑day oversold reading without confirming that the price has **stabilized** (e.g., via a higher‑timeframe support level) can lead to premature entries.  
- **Position sizing**: No guidance on **maximum allocation per ticker** or **overall exposure** is provided, despite the low conviction. This omission can lead to over‑weighting speculative bets.
